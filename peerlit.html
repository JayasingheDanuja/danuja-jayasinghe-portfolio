PeerLit is a cross-platform mobile app built on Flutter that creates a digital ecosystem where university students can find study partners, stay accountable through social features, and engage in immersive learning environments. It was built as part of the Software Development Group Project module and entered into Hult Prize 2026 and Codesprint 11.

Within the team, I was responsible for the entire UI design system, the mascot character design, and frontend development of the landing page, smart global search, and peer matching interface. The product went from concept to a live demo-ready state.

The problem we solved: Students study in silos. Existing tools like Notion, Pomodoro apps, and group chats are fragmented. PeerLit brings peer matching, accountability, AI assistance, and 3D study spaces into one coherent experience.

Mascot Design

The character that holds it together

One of the most unexpected parts of this project was designing a mascot. The character needed to feel approachable, slightly academic, and playful without being childish. It had to work both as a flat 2D illustration in the app and as a reference for the 3D Unity avatar the game dev team built.

The mascot appears throughout the onboarding flow, in the gamification badges, and as customizable avatars in the 3D study space, giving the app a cohesive personality that distinguishes it from generic productivity tools.

Key Features

Four pillars of the product

Focus Mode
AI Companion
Gamification

Social Focus Mode: The most praised feature in user testing. When a user activates focus mode, the app locks for a set duration. If they break focus early, their entire study group is instantly notified. The peer pressure is intentional and it works.

AI Study Companion: A Python-based assistant powered by Google Gemini. Users upload notes for summarization, ask questions via Socratic dialogue, and generate practice quizzes. Response times measured in milliseconds during testing.

Results

Validation and recognition

User feedback highlighted that the Social Focus Mode created a sense of responsibility that solo study tools fundamentally lack. Qualitative evaluation praised the navigation as natural and straightforward, allowing users to move between forums, 3D rooms, and chat without any guidance.

Reflections

What I learned

01
Designing for a developer handoff is a different skill. Every decision I made in Figma had to be buildable by my teammates in Flutter. That constraint made me a more precise designer.
02
Mascot design is harder than it looks. A character that works at 24px in a badge AND at full screen in a 3D Unity environment requires thinking about form at very different scales simultaneously.
03
A strong UI system saves everyone time. Because I built a thorough component library early, the team could build consistently without asking me to review every screen. Upfront investment in system thinking paid off.
