EcoX is a mobile AR and AI application conceived for a world where the boundaries between physical and digital have dissolved. The brief asked: design an app that enhances life in a blended-reality 2050 city. My answer was a platform that makes environmental data visible through augmented reality and turns passive urban residents into active sustainability participants.

This was a group participation project, but the entire UI/UX design was my responsibility. Every screen, every interaction, every visual decision was mine. It is also my most visually ambitious work to date, earning a competition certificate for design quality.

The design challenge: How do you make abstract data like CO2 levels and noise pollution feel personal, urgent, and actionable without overwhelming users in their everyday environment?

The Problem

Data exists. People do not feel it.

Modern cities generate enormous amounts of environmental data: air quality readings, noise pollution maps, carbon footprint trackers. But this information is buried in government portals, academic reports, or apps that require effort to seek out. The result is that most urban residents feel disconnected from the environment they live in and unmotivated to change their behavior.

The brief challenged me to design for a 2050 scenario where AR is ambient, AI is personal, and sustainability is a community behavior. The question became: what does eco-conscious urban life actually look like when the city itself becomes your interface?

Visual Design

A palette that feels alive

The visual language needed to feel organic yet technological. Eco Green grounds the interface in nature and health. Clean Sky references open air and possibility. White space is generous to prevent the AR data overlays from feeling claustrophobic. The design uses Poppins for its geometric clarity, which pairs well with data-heavy screens.

Key Features

Four features. One connected experience.

Dynamic Emissions Overlay
AR scanner that visualizes real-time CO2, PM2.5, and noise pollution as heat maps layered onto the physical environment. Users see their city's invisible health data the moment they open the camera.
AI Canopy Route Navigation
An adaptive routing system that recommends the healthiest, greenest walking or commuting route based on current pollution levels, shade cover, and available greenery.
Waste-to-Resource AR Guide
Point your camera at any waste item for instant identification and 3D step-by-step disassembly and disposal instructions. Circular economy made effortless.
Eco-Scenario Builder
A co-design tool where citizens can place 3D models of urban improvements like rooftop gardens and solar panels onto real locations and visualize their sustainability impact before anything is built.

Accessibility & Inclusivity

Designed for all humans

The brief specifically asked for an interface that ensures all humans can experience life without limits. This pushed me to make deliberate accessibility decisions throughout the design.

01
High-contrast AR overlays ensure pollution data is legible outdoors in bright sunlight, not just on a dark studio monitor.
02
Voice-over functionality built into the navigation flow ensures users with visual limitations can still interact with AR features through audio cues.
03
Color-blind compliant visuals using tested palette combinations so that the eco health indicators are readable regardless of color perception.
04
Gesture and voice multimodal input means users are never locked into a single interaction method, accommodating a wide range of physical abilities.

Reflections

What I learned designing for the future

01
Designing without existing patterns is freeing but disorienting. There are no established conventions for AR sustainability apps, which meant every interaction had to be justified from first principles.
02
Visual ambition has to serve clarity. The most beautiful screens in this project are also the most usable. When I chased visual complexity without a functional reason, the screens broke down.
03
Gamification and community are more motivating than guilt. Eco-Stamps and public leaderboards create social proof and positive reinforcement, which research shows drives behavior change better than warnings about climate impact.
